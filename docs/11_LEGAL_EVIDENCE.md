# Legal meaning, evidence and permission boundaries

US-oriented product design notes researched 2026-09-11, not a legal opinion for any particular transaction. Other jurisdictions, minor contributors, labor arrangements and publisher contracts can change the analysis. Primary sources are [S29–S38](../shared/RESEARCH_SOURCES.md).

## What a record may support

A retained file matching a signed digest, a public chain observation, a chosen name, exact statement and acknowledged terms may support chronology, integrity, attribution and agreement evidence. Authentication still matters. A timestamp proves neither original authorship nor superior legal title. A private key proves signing capability, not automatically the flesh-and-blood claimant. Server-custodied keys weaken claims that only the individual could have signed. [S35–S37]

Copyright in qualifying original expression generally begins when fixed, not when hashed; “poor man's copyright” is not a substitute for registration. Transfers of copyright ownership have separate writing/signature requirements, and legal effect depends on the underlying transaction. Tessera should not represent that a revenue percentage transfers copyright. [S29–S31]

## Required agreements, not premature boilerplate

The implementation must preserve an explicit contributor agreement covering permitted artifact use, derivative/branch rights, scope/territory/duration where relevant, economic-base definition, normalized payouts, use of prior contributions, withdrawal/amendment, attribution, disputes and liability representations. A project using third-party material also needs whatever underlying licenses are actually required.

A name field stays simple. Do not demand legal identity on-chain or publish IDs. For disputes, a participant may later supply account history, drafts, correspondence and external verification. Maintain privacy-conscious consent records and export them to the participant when appropriate; do not make a public surveillance archive under the label of evidence.

An application should not silently create a copyright assignment, exclusive license, investment/security or employment promise by choosing a default schema value. Those are separate legally consequential options requiring carefully defined terms and review. The tokenless revenue-allocation MVP avoids unnecessary speculative-finance features; it does not guarantee exemption from every law.

## Persistence versus survivability of rights

The records can outlast a particular server. That does not establish that licenses, payment obligations or rights survive bankruptcy, acquisition or contract termination unchanged. Nor does it make funds appear if a platform failed to remit them. User-facing wording: “Portable signed contribution history and economic allocation records,” not “rights protected forever even after bankruptcy.”

Electronic agreement does not need a future blockchain statute merely to be potentially meaningful, but existing contract/evidence requirements still apply. E-SIGN does not certify every signed JSON object. Keep full readable terms, affirmative consent and signer-context evidence rather than relying on legal-sounding status badges. [S35–S37]

## Name and IP risk

The founder has chosen Tessera. Use it consistently in this packet, but do not purchase expensive brand assets or represent clearance: existing blockchain infrastructure and another on-chain business already use the exact name [S39,S40]. That is a material knockout-search flag, not a conclusion about infringement or registrability. Both Tessera and Morpheus need current trademark/domain/package review before public brand investment. Agents may research conflicts and prepare a report; they must not silently rename either product.

Software licensing is also a decision: permissive open-source Tessera is a plausible adoption/public-goods strategy, while Morpheus may have a different license. Do not insert an irrevocable public license or publish private source without founder authorization. Record third-party dependency/license obligations and creative demo permissions.
